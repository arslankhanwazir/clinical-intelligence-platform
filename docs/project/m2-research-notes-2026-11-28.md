# NORTHSTAR M2 research notes - refreshed 2026-11-28

**Research cutoff:** 28 November 2026. **Jurisdiction focus:** Pakistan. This is public-source desk research, not legal advice, clinical validation or a DRAP determination.

## 1. Current clinical-source landscape

### Pakistan national candidate

The Pakistan Hypertension League's 4th National Hypertension Guidelines remains a relevant Pakistan-specific candidate identified in this desk review. Its citation/issue metadata is inconsistent across primary sources: the guideline PDF cites *J Pak Med Assoc* 2023;73(Suppl 7):S1-78 and its cover shows Vol. 73 No. 8 (Suppl. 7), August 2023, while JPMA's archive labels the issue Vol. 73 No. 7 and states publication 2023-08-09. Keep the discrepancy open for publisher confirmation; do not silently normalize it.

The guideline-specific disclaimer says contents are copyrighted by Pakistan Hypertension League and the committee/authors and allows use without permission for academic and non-commercial activities with attribution. JPMA's general open-access policy describes CC BY 4.0. The interaction between the supplement-specific disclaimer and general journal policy is unresolved for commercial product reuse. Obtain written clarification from the publisher and rights holders before any commercial ingestion, reproduction, adaptation or embedding.

### New global candidate available online before the issue date

The 2026 International Society of Hypertension Global Hypertension Practice Guidelines (DOI 10.1097/HJH.0000000000004451) has an article date of 2026-10-20 / online-ahead-of-print record. PubMed lists the issue publication date as 2026-12-01, which is after this research cutoff. Record it as an online-ahead-of-print candidate as of 2026-11-28, not as a completed December issue. It is a global guideline, not a Pakistan national guideline. Full version, corrections, exact applicable sections and commercial reuse rights must be verified before approval. Open/free access is not proof of unrestricted commercial reuse.

### WHO reference

WHO's *Guideline for the pharmacological treatment of hypertension in adults* was published 2021-08-24 and targets appropriately diagnosed non-pregnant adults. It is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO; WHO's rights policy states commercial use requires permission. It is a candidate for reviewer context only. Its pharmacological recommendations are outside the current v1 boundary that excludes medication selection/change recommendations.

## 2. Updated DRAP screen

1. DRAP's “Is It a Medical Device?” page (last updated 2025-07-01) expressly includes software where the manufacturer's intended purpose is one or more specified medical purposes. Do not infer an exemption from the proposed human-review or non-prescribing boundaries.
2. The DRAP-hosted Medical Devices Rules, 2017 provide four device classes (A-D). Schedule A states that standalone software falling within the medical-device definition is treated as an active device and classified in its own right using the rules. The project does not assign a class.
3. The actual S.R.O. 1049(I)/2025 notification changes Rule 19(3) by adding an emergency supply-priority proviso for registration holders during war/national emergency. It does not, on its face, decide software intended-purpose or SaMD classification. Counsel should still check its application to any future registration holder.
4. DRAP's medical-device guidance index reports last update 2026-05-05 and visibly lists general guidance (including good distribution practices, reliance and appeals). No dedicated standalone-software/SaMD document was visible in that index during this search; this is not proof that no separate direction or unpublished/other guidance exists.
5. DRAP's Medical Device Board page lists decisions from its 136th-139th meetings dated 2026-08-28. The individual documents were not reviewed here; regulatory counsel must screen them for relevance.
6. DRAP lists S.R.O. 1450(I)/2026, Medical Devices (Refurbishment) Regulations, 2026, dated 2026-09-03. There is no apparent direct fit to the software-only v1 concept; counsel must confirm if any hardware/refurbished device scope is later added.
7. DRAP's medical-device update list records development of a Medical Devices Module on Pakistan Single Window for import clearance on 2026-10-17. This appears to be an import-administration update, not a software-classification route. It may matter only if regulated hardware import enters scope.

## 3. M2 conclusions that are supportable now

- The intended purpose needs formal Pakistan regulatory assessment; no exemption or device class can be asserted by the project team.
- PHL, WHO and ISH materials are candidates only; commercial reuse is not cleared for the sources presently identified.
- The source/version policy, exclusions, hazard register and rule approval workflow can be prepared as drafts, but only a qualified clinician can approve their clinical content and risk ratings.
- The 13-item preliminary hazard register is appropriate for initial identification; no severity, likelihood or residual-risk scores have been invented.
- The safety-case document is a skeleton. None of its claims is yet substantiated.

## 4. What remains impossible without external parties

- Qualified clinician selection and engagement.
- Clinical approval of scope, population, exclusions, source selection, data requirements, fallback and hazard ratings.
- Written commercial permissions / rights clarification from PHL/JPMA, WHO and any other publisher whose material might be used.
- Regulatory counsel's written opinion and any DRAP clarification or determination.
- Site/institution approval and current-state clinic workflow observation.

## 5. Evidence limitations

- No clinical recommendations, thresholds, expected clinical outputs or rule logic were extracted into NORTHSTAR.
- No clinician or regulator has reviewed or approved this work package.
- The Medical Device Board 136th-139th meeting decision files and the 2026 refurbishment instrument have not been analysed clause-by-clause in this desk note.
- The current DRAP search is a public-source screening, not an exhaustive consolidated-law search.

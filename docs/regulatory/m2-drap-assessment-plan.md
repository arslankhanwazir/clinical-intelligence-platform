# M2 DRAP / regulatory assessment plan

**Jurisdiction:** Pakistan (initial market hypothesis)
**Status:** Preliminary desk research; no legal opinion, DRAP determination, device class or exemption is claimed.

## 1. Regulatory facts identified from official sources

- DRAP's “Is It a Medical Device?” page, last updated 1 July 2025, includes software in the definition where the manufacturer's intended purpose is one or more specified medical purposes, including diagnosis, prevention, monitoring or treatment of disease.
- DRAP-hosted Medical Devices Rules, 2017 include a four-class framework (A–D). The rules state that standalone software within the medical-device definition is considered an active device and is classified in its own right; classification depends on intended use and the relevant rules. This project does not assign a class.
- DRAP lists an amendment to Rule 19 of the Medical Devices Rules, 2017 as S.R.O. 1049(I)/2025, dated 11 June 2025. Its complete effect on this product has not been assessed here.
- DRAP's medical-device guideline index was last updated 5 May 2026 in the retrieved page. Each potentially applicable guidance document must be checked individually.

## 2. Why NORTHSTAR needs formal assessment

The proposed product is clinician-facing and organizes patient context, surfaces rule/evidence results and prepares a source-linked draft in an adult hypertension follow-up workflow. Its intended purpose and the way clinicians rely on its outputs may be relevant to the medical-device definition. Excluding autonomous diagnosis or prescribing does not, by itself, establish that the software is outside regulation. Obtain a written assessment from qualified Pakistan regulatory counsel and/or DRAP through the appropriate route.

## 3. Questions for counsel / DRAP

1. Based on the exact intended-use statement, outputs, user population and workflow, does NORTHSTAR fall within the medical-device definition under the DRAP Act/Medical Devices Rules?
2. Is the product standalone medical-device software under the current rules, or does another classification apply?
3. Which current classification rules, schedules and amendments apply to this intended use? What class, if any, is indicated, and does DRAP need to resolve an interpretation issue?
4. What establishment licensing, product enlistment/registration, technical documentation, quality-management, performance/clinical-evaluation and labelling obligations apply before deployment or marketing?
5. What obligations apply to software updates, model changes, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, hosting/on-premises deployment, integration with an HIS/EHR and post-market monitoring/reporting?
6. What evidence, local representative/manufacturer arrangements, records and approvals are required for a synthetic demonstration, supervised evaluation and any future clinical pilot?
7. Which other Pakistani federal/provincial health, privacy, professional and institutional requirements apply to the intended deployment and data flows?
8. Which written determination, pre-submission consultation or DRAP contact route should be used, and who will own the action?

## 4. Evidence package to give the adviser

- Current product brief, requirements and intended-use draft.
- Proposed workflow map, explicitly marked unobserved.
- Exact planned features and prohibited functions.
- User roles and intended users' qualifications.
- Data sources, inputs, outputs, automation level and clinician reliance description.
- Architecture/data-flow and hosting/deployment concept when available.
- Source/licensing register and preliminary hazard register.
- Commercial model and intended markets; distinguish Pakistan from later jurisdictions.
